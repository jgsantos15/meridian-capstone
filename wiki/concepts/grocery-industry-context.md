# Grocery Industry Context

National grocery-sector trends and competitive dynamics relevant to Meridian's specialty-grocery positioning and future site-selection decisions.

**Sources:** `raw/icsc-grocery-sector-trends` (Commerce + Communities Today, April 24, 2026), `raw/grocerydive-2025-trends-forecast-grading` (Grocery Dive, December 23, 2025), `raw/jll-grocery-tracker` (JLL Grocery Report 2025, February 18, 2025)

## Market size & investment appetite
- The U.S. grocery sector is valued at **$912.4 billion** (IBISWorld).
- Deals for grocery-anchored centers totaled **almost $11 billion in 2025, up 42% from 2024** (JLL Grocery Tracker 2026); the prior year's JLL data already showed $7.0 billion in multi-tenant grocery-anchored transactions for 2024, at a then-record **$209/sq. ft.** average price — the two years together show accelerating investor appetite, not a one-off spike.
- Naveen Jaggi (JLL): "The days when any grocery store could anchor a center equally well are fading" — specialty and value-oriented formats are reshaping the landscape, not generic grocery anchors. (Jaggi is JLL's President of Retail Advisory Services — the same source behind the 2025 Grocery Report below.)

## Site availability & real estate economics — directly relevant to the Pasadena decision
- Grocery-anchored retail vacancy compressed to **3.5% in Q4 2024** — 100 basis points below the pandemic-era peak, and net new supply has run under 100,000 sq. ft. nationally for two straight years (JLL). Available space is scarce and getting scarcer.
- Grocery-anchored retail posted **3.1% annual rent growth in Q4 2024** — the highest of any retail subtype tracked, ahead of neighborhood centers and power centers (both 2.9%).
- JLL's own outlook: "demand-supply mismatch expected to persist... landlords positioned for continued rent growth." For a chain evaluating a new lease in Pasadena, this is a live cost and timing pressure, not background noise — the longer the decision takes, the more expensive or scarce a good site is likely to become.

## Specialty/value bifurcation — where Meridian sits
- Trader Joe's grew **3% year-over-year**, 6 points above the sector average, with "double-digit year-over-year growth among Gen Z shoppers" (Consumer Edge).
- Traditional grocers (Publix, Safeway) are "seeing pullbacks from every income group, with the sharpest declines among lower-income shoppers" (Consumer Edge).
- Discount grocery (Aldi, Food 4 Less) growth has **plateaued since mid-2025**.
- Michael Gunther (Consumer Edge): shoppers gravitate to retailers offering "a clear reason to be loyal, whether that's unbeatable value at a hard discounter or a curated private-label experience." Meridian's stated positioning — prepared foods, local sourcing, smaller footprint than national chains — reads as a bet on the "curated" side of that split, not the discount side.

## A close comparable: Gelson's (Southern California)
- Gelson's operates **27 traditional grocery stores in Southern California** — the closest scale/geography comparable to Meridian's 14 stores across LA/OC/Ventura.
- Gelson's is piloting small-format "ReCharge by Gelson's" micromarkets (3,000–5,400 sq. ft.) paired with EV charging, in Costa Mesa and Santa Ana.
- Worth understanding in the interview whether Dana considers Gelson's a direct competitor or a format Meridian is watching.

## Health & wellness tailwind for specialty grocers
- Grocery Dive's year-end 2025 review graded this trend an **A**: specialty grocers "post[ed] sales increases as consumers sought them for nutritional expertise" amid a broader health/wellness push (the "Make America Healthy Again" movement, added-protein/low-sugar product growth, states restricting junk food from SNAP eligibility). This isn't just Meridian's own strategy — it's a sector-wide tailwind specialty positioning is riding in 2025.

## Private label — a trend Meridian doesn't appear to be riding yet
- National private-label sales topped **$270 billion in 2024** (+3.4% YoY), with market share at **20.7%** (up 160 bps since 2021); FMI reports 86% of retailer/manufacturer executives plan to increase private-label investment over the next two years (JLL; Grocery Dive).
- The client brief doesn't mention Meridian carrying any private-label line. Worth asking whether that's a deliberate choice (consistent with a "curated, sourced" positioning) or an unexplored opportunity.

## Restaurant-vs-grocery substitution — two independent data points agree
- JLL: the spending gap between dining out and grocery shopping widened to **over $21 billion by the end of 2024**, from $3.4 billion in January 2022.
- Separately, per Tillster's 2026 Phygital Index Report (2,144 diners surveyed): 36% visit grocery stores more often and 33% visit convenience stores more often, while fast-food visits are down for 29% of respondents and fast-casual visits are down for 37%.
- Two independent sources, a year apart, point the same direction: grocery — prepared foods especially — is picking up share from restaurant spend, which lines up directly with Meridian's prepared-foods positioning.

## Traditional grocers under pressure, specialty/discount pulling ahead
- Grocery Dive's 2025 review (Grade: B): Albertsons and Kroger closed stores and cut costs — Kroger "sharply scaled back its automated fulfillment network" — while "specialty chains like Sprouts Farmers Market and discounters including Aldi continued to grow rapidly."
- JLL foot-traffic data backs this up: Trader Joe's visits were up **24.7% versus 2019**, Aldi up **51.2%**, Grocery Outlet up **48.7%** — all specialty/discount formats outgrowing the broader industry's 10.9% five-year foot-traffic growth.
- Sprouts (35 new stores, ~840K sq. ft.) and Trader Joe's (34 new stores, ~450K sq. ft.) were both among the fastest-expanding grocers nationally in 2024 — specialty format growth, not just discount growth.

## Investment activity in Meridian's own markets
- Brixton Capital acquired Quad at Whittier (Vallarta Supermarkets-anchored, Los Angeles) for **$100 million** (314,593 sq. ft., 95.6% leased) — a grocery-anchored center in one of Meridian's own counties, signaling continued investor appetite for grocery-anchored retail in the LA market specifically.

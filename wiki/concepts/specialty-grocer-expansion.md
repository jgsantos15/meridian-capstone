# Specialty Grocer Expansion

Where specialty grocers are opening today — national store-count growth by chain, and what's happening specifically in Meridian's Southern California market. Filed in response to the research question: "What do our sources say about where specialty grocers are opening?"

**Sources:** `raw/jll-grocery-tracker`, `raw/icsc-grocery-sector-trends`, `raw/grocerydive-2025-trends-forecast-grading`

## National store-count growth (2024), by chain
- **Sprouts Farmers Market** — 35 new stores (~840,000 sq. ft.); entered **Wyoming** for the first time; expanded presence in **Delaware, South Carolina, and Virginia**; new-store openings up 16.7% year-over-year. (`raw/jll-grocery-tracker`)
- **Trader Joe's** — 34 new locations (450,000+ sq. ft.) nationally; 3% year-over-year sales growth for the period ending Feb. 28 — 6 points above the sector average, with "double-digit year-over-year growth among Gen Z shoppers" (Consumer Edge). (`raw/jll-grocery-tracker`, `raw/icsc-grocery-sector-trends`)
- **The Fresh Market** — 8 new locations (~200,000 sq. ft.). (`raw/jll-grocery-tracker`)

## Southern California — Meridian's own market
- **Gelson's** (27 traditional stores in Southern California) is piloting a smaller "ReCharge by Gelson's" micromarket format: first location in **Santa Ana** (3,000 sq. ft.), second in **Costa Mesa** (5,400 sq. ft.), each paired with EV charging. (`raw/icsc-grocery-sector-trends`)
- **Whole Foods** is anchoring a new 71,400-sq.-ft. Regency Centers development in **Redlands, California** (36,000-sq.-ft. store plus four additional retail buildings and a drive-thru pad). (`raw/icsc-grocery-sector-trends`)
- Brixton Capital paid **$100M** for Quad at Whittier in **Los Angeles**, a center anchored by Vallarta Supermarkets (a specialty/Latino-format grocer) — signaling continued investor appetite for grocery-anchored retail in Meridian's home county. (`raw/icsc-grocery-sector-trends`)

## Sector-level framing
- Grocery Dive's 2025 year-end review: specialty chains "like Sprouts Farmers Market" (and discounters like Aldi) "continued to grow rapidly" in 2025, contrasted with traditional grocers (Albertsons, Kroger) that closed stores and cut costs. (`raw/grocerydive-2025-trends-forecast-grading`)
- Nationally, grocery-anchored retail vacancy is at 3.5% (Q4 2024) with net new supply under 100,000 sq. ft. for two straight years running — available space for any new opening, specialty included, is scarce. (`raw/jll-grocery-tracker`)

## Demand-side signals: why specialty is growing (Grocery Dive, 2025)
Grocery Dive says nothing about *where* chains are opening — the location evidence above comes from JLL and ICSC. What it adds is the demand side:
- **Health & wellness (graded A):** specialty grocers "post[ed] sales increases as consumers sought them for 'nutritional expertise'" amid the "Make America Healthy Again" push, added-protein/low-sugar products, and state limits on "junk" food in SNAP. This is the clearest source-stated reason specialty formats are gaining. (`raw/grocerydive-2025-trends-forecast-grading`)
- **Private label at a specialty chain:** Natural Grocers reported "stronger net sales" largely due to its "fast-growing private label assortment," which it links "to its loyalty program." Grocery Dive also notes households earning above $100,000 are "becoming more likely to buy private label groceries." (`raw/grocerydive-2025-trends-forecast-grading`)
- **Value alone isn't a guarantee:** Grocery Outlet "struggled to connect with shoppers" despite its discount focus, while Aldi and Sprouts kept growing. (`raw/grocerydive-2025-trends-forecast-grading`)

## My own conclusion (not stated directly by the sources)
Specialty grocers appear to be expanding along two tracks at once — geographic push into states where they have little or no presence (Sprouts into Wyoming/Delaware/South Carolina/Virginia), and format experimentation in already-saturated home markets (Gelson's micromarkets in Orange County) rather than more full-size stores. For Meridian, the closer analog is the second pattern — a same-market, smaller-footprint play in a supply-constrained region — which is directly relevant context for the Pasadena decision.

A second inference, from the Grocery Dive material: Natural Grocers pairing a private-label push with its loyalty program is a specialty-grocer playbook Meridian could in principle copy — Meridian has ~40,000 loyalty members whose data it has "never really used" (see [Data Sources](data-sources.md)) and no known private-label line (see [Grocery Industry Context](grocery-industry-context.md)). Whether that's a fit is a question for Dana, not something the sources establish.
